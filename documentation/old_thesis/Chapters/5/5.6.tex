\section{Validation}
This section undergoes a validation procedure, in which both mass conservation and BOXM chemical evolution are validated, to confirm that GPAT is producing sensible outputs that align with physical laws and previous model data.

\subsection{Mass Conversation}
This test ensures that emission mass is conserved after the plume aggregation process is carried out. This compares the total mass of all geo-vector plume segments at all time steps to the aggregated mass on the Eulerian grid.

\subsubsection{Methodology}
The total vector mass (\ce{m_{vec}}) is derived directly from the GPAT Plume Dataset, as the sum of all plume segment masses at each time step:
\begin{equation}
    \ce{m_{vec,tot} [kg]} = \sum m_{vec,plume} [kg]
\end{equation}
Emissions are aggregated to the grid in concentration form, meaning to calculate the total grid mass (\ce{m_{grid}}), the polluted grid cell concentrations (\ce{c_{grid}}) in ppbv have to be multiplied by the dimensions of grid cells (hres, vres) to calculate the mass in kilograms. 

Firstly, the concentration is converted from ppbv to molec./cm\textsuperscript{3}, using Avogadro's constant ($N_A$) and the molar mass of emission species being converted ($MM$): 
\begin{equation}
    \ce{c_{grid,cell} [\ce{molec/cm^3}]} = \frac{\ce{c_{grid,cell} [\ce{ppbv}]} \times [\ce{M}] \times MM}{N_A \times 1 \times 10^9}
\end{equation}
then to kg/m\textsuperscript{3}:
\begin{equation}
    \ce{c_{grid,cell} [\ce{kg/m^3}]} = \ce{c_{grid,cell} [\ce{molec/cm^3}]} \times 1 \times 10^3
\end{equation}
Following this, concentration is converted to mass through multiplication by grid cell volume (i.e.horizontal and vertical resolutions in metres):
\begin{equation}
    \ce{m_{grid,cell} [kg]} = \ce{c_{grid,cell} [\ce{kg/m^3}]} \times \ce{vres [\ce{m}]} \times \ce{hres_{dlat} [\ce{m}]} \times \ce{hres_{dlon} [\ce{m}]}
\end{equation}
Lastly, the total grid mass is calculated as the sum of all cell masses at each time step:
\begin{equation}
    \ce{m_{grid,tot} [kg]} = \sum \ce{m_{grid,cell} [kg]}
\end{equation}
The mass conservation variable (mc) is calculated as the percentage mass conserved converting vector mass to grid mass.

The two dependent variables to investigate were: total wall time of the run (wall\_time) and percentage mass conserved (mc). The base case run is shown in the first data entry row of table \ref{mc_v_table}. All other rows show groups of runs which correspond to the variation of each parameter. This mass conservation study was the first set of simulations conducted using the GPAT architecture. The purpose was to investigate how the plume aggregation scheme worked under a range of different scenarios in zero wind conditions. 
The base case was a scenario with a two-aircraft configuration separated by 1,000~m along the flight direction. The plume aggregation was performed with 10 Gaussian slices for each plume segment, a maximum plume age of six hours and horizontal resolution of 0.05\textdegree. The following parameters were set in sim\_params:
\begin{itemize}
    \item lat\_bounds: (0.0, 1.0)
    \item lon\_bounds: (0.0, 1.0)
    \item alt\_bounds: (10000, 11000)
    \item vres\_sim: 500
    \item species\_in: (``NO'', ``NO2'', ``CO'', ``HCHO'', ``CH3CHO'', ``C2H4'', ``C3H6'', ``C2H2'', ``BENZENE'')
    \item species\_out: (``O3'', ``NO2'', ``NO'', ``NO3'', ``HNO3'', ``PAN'', ``HONO'', ``HO2'', ``OH'', ``H2O2'', ``CO'', ``HCHO'', ``CH4'', ``CH3O2'')
\end{itemize}

In the first mass conservation test, the number of aircraft in the simulation is varied as the sensitivity parameter. This did not go quite as expected, as the 5 and 10 aircraft runs did not complete within a six-hour wall time. Next was the test to vary heading angle. This was the most pertinent of tests as it showed that flying aircraft at a 90\textdegree \ heading led to both a reduction in run time and an 11\% increase in plume-to-grid mass conservation. Therefore, seeing as orientation was not a necessary factor to vary further, 90\textdegree \ heading was selected for studies presented in chapter \ref{chapter6}.

Following this, a range of separation distances were tested, showing a significant increase in run time and $\approx$5\% decrease in accuracy of plume aggregation, when the aircraft are separated by a further distance. It is not entirely obvious what caused this, and is a focus for future work on validating this modelling framework.

The remaining three tests were for varying no\_slices, max\_age and hres. Varying the number of slices led to no change in accuracy, but significantly increased wall time with increasing slice number. It was not obvious how this would change under different numbers of aircraft and orientations, so no\_slices was kept at 10 for the remaining studies in chapter \ref{chapter6}. Max plume age showed a trend of increasing wall time and decreasing accuracy, however to sufficiently capture subgrid scale effects, max\_age was kept to 6~h. The horizontal resolution variation led to expected increases in wall time and accuracy with increasing resolution. 0.025\textdegree \ and 0.01\textdegree \ did not finish in the 6~h window, so 0.05\textdegree \ was used in all following studies as a suitable compromise.

\subsection{Box Model Outputs}
This validation study investigated the accuracy of the new vectorised photochemical trajectory model, BOXM v2.0, against its predecessor, BOXM v1.0. It was decided to run this test alongside the background study in section \ref{background_study}. This included a run for every month across five unique hotspot locations (figure \ref{hotspot_locations}). These runs were 5 days long, and did not incorporate any emissions into the model. The species output from the model were investigated for their correlation with the outputs from the equivalent run in BOXM v1.0.

\subsubsection{Methodology}
To test how similar the two sets of time series outputs were for each run, the coefficient of determination ($R^2$) was calculated as follows:

\begin{equation}
    R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \bar{y})^2}
\end{equation}

where $y_i$ is the BOXM v1.0 output concentration and $\hat{y}_i$ is the BOXM v2.0 output concentration for the selected species at a particular time step. $\bar{y}$ is the mean of the BOXM v1.0 output concentrations across all time steps for a selected species and $n$ is the number of time steps in the simulation.

\subsubsection{BOXM Validation Study}
The outputs from all key species concentrations across the five-day model run are presented here. Figure \ref{tab:average_r2_results} shows a very strong correlation between BOXM v1.0 and BOXM v2.0 through the averaged $R^2$ values. The correlations averaged above 99\% across all species and all runs, thus the reimplementation of the photochemical box model was successful, and well within the bounds of rounding errors.
